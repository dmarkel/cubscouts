Your Scout Is Working on Bobcat!

Dear Webelos families,

Our den is starting the year with Bobcat, the first required adventure for the Webelos rank. In our den meetings, scouts are getting to know each other, learning what the Scout Oath and Scout Law mean, writing our den's Code of Conduct, learning about the denner (a rotating youth leader), and practicing the Cub Scout sign, salute, and handshake.

We need your help with one requirement at home

Requirement 7: At home, with your parent or legal guardian, do the activities in the booklet How to Protect Your Children from Child Abuse: A Parent's Guide.

  • Where to find it: The booklet is in the front of your scout's Webelos Handbook. You can also find it on Scouting America's website by searching for the title. If you need a copy, just ask me.
  • What it is: A short guide to help you talk with your child about personal safety, including how to recognize, respond to, and report situations that don't feel right, with age-appropriate activities you do together.
  • Why at home: You know your child best. These conversations are most effective coming from a parent, at a time and place where your child feels comfortable.
  • How long: Plan on 20–30 minutes. Many families split it into a couple of short talks.

Tips for the conversation

  • Pick a relaxed time, like a car ride or bedtime. Keep it calm and matter-of-fact.
  • Read the parent section first, then do the activities with your scout.
  • Let your scout know they can always come to you, and that they won't be in trouble for telling.
  • Help your scout name a few other trusted adults they could talk to.

Dinner-table quiz: Ask your scout to say the Scout Law and tell you which point they think is most important. Then ask them to show you the Cub Scout handshake!

Thank you for being part of your scout's Scouting journey. Questions? Please reach out any time.   Yours in Scouting,
